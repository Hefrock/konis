Iowa Lead Azide Detonation


Update: Iowa Lead Azide Successfully Destroyed

No measurable lead levels headed to Missouri.

Wednesday, Nov. 25, at about 9 am, the U.S. Army successfully completed a controlled, mitigated detonation of the lead azide stored at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. Middleton is 57 miles north of the Missouri state line and 105 miles from Hannibal, Missouri.

  • Iowa Army Ammunition Plant Update Press Conference, Nov. 24, 2026
  • Site Preparation Video - The federal multi-agency team planning the detonation is taking unprecedented measures to both contain the explosion and limit the dispersion of lead into the environment. These efforts include layers of earth, sand, water and two feet of high-expansion foam in addition to the concrete bunker in which the detonation will occur. 

Lead azide is an explosive compound that contains lead. The Army found 68 drums of lead azide stored at the Iowa plant had degraded and become unstable. The Army concluded the material could not be safely handled or moved, so it destroyed it in place with a controlled detonation authorized under a U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)-issued emergency permit. 

Post Detonation Monitoring

EPA is in Middletown testing air, water and soil in the area before, during and after the detonation. Beginning immediately after the blast, EPA began intensive air sampling in the area nearest the detonation site. Currently, EPA reports no detectable traces of lead heading toward Missouri and does not expect any traceable lead levels to reach the state.

Soil and Surface Water Testing Requests

Requests for soil and surface water sampling should be directed to EPA at 833-649-9624. If you are requesting sampling, please clearly state that you have a sampling request and provide your name, phone number, and address.

For additional questions or concerns, please continue to email R7-IAAAP@epa.gov.

Missouri's Response

On Nov. 20, Gov. Mike Kehoe activated a multi-state agency team to carry out a comprehensive plan to monitor air, water, agriculture and public health in northeast Missouri in response to the planned detonation. Governor Kehoe and state officials from the Department of Natural Resources, Department of Health and Senior Services, Department of Agriculture, and the Department of Public Safety and other agencies, maintained communication with the White House and key federal and other partners. Those entities included the U.S. Army, the Federal Emergency Management Agency, EPA Region 7, U.S. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Administration for Strategic Preparedness and Response, U.S. Department of Agriculture, U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and Iowa officials.

The Missouri National Guard also provided technical expertise to assist state agencies in assessing potential impacts to Missouri.

While there are no detectable traces of lead heading to Missouri and no anticipated impact to air, water, agriculture and public health, state officials will continue to evaluate post-detonation findings and provide relevant information to Missourians as it becomes available.

Natural Resources

The Missouri Department of Natural Resources is working closely with state and federal partners to assess and respond to any environmental impacts after the detonation.

  • Impacts to the state depend on weather conditions at the time of detonation. We do not anticipate sustained concentrations of lead at levels that threaten human health and the environment. 
  • To ensure drinking water provided to the public has not been impacted, the department has partnered with communities to conduct monitoring at primary public drinking water systems that use surface water. Missouri has 10 drinking water systems and 13 intakes within a 100-mile radius of the detonation site. We have been in contact with the 10 systems to make sure they are aware of the planned detonation event. 
  • All surface water systems have treatment processes in place that can address lead as part of their normal operations. Enhanced monitoring will allow the systems to make any necessary treatment adjustments to ensure safe drinking water continues to be provided.    
  • The Missouri Department of Natural Resources stands ready to assist any community in need. Our team will continue to monitor changing weather patterns and be prepared to offer assistance to impacted systems.

Health and Senior Services

The Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services is working closely with partners at every level to assess any potential impacts this detonation event may have on human health.

The anticipated risk to public health in Missouri is low.

We expect this to be an isolated, short-term event. Negative impacts of lead exposure are typically due to long-term, chronic exposures.

Guidance for Individuals and Communities

For those who are concerned about exposure, the Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services advises the following additional precautions: 

  • Stay indoors with pets.
  • Keep windows closed.
  • Close fresh air intakes in homes and vehicles for 24 to 48 hours after detonation.
  • Park vehicles in closed garage if possible
  • Bring children’s toys, bikes and toys inside
  • Relocate pet food, water bowls, etc.
  • Cover outdoor furniture, playground equipment, sandboxes, etc.

There are no safety recommendations for individuals and communities outside of the downwind path. Anyone with concerns about possible exposure to lead dust should contact their local public health agency or healthcare provider to discuss. For additional information, review the department's Public Health Guidance: Ammunition Detonation in Iowa.

Agriculture

The potential risk to Missouri’s livestock, crop and agricultural land is very low. Much of this year’s field crops have been harvested with the growing season already over. The Missouri Department of Agriculture is encouraging producers in northeast Missouri that are concerned about their livestock to work with their local veterinarian to create a plan that works best for their farm before the detonation, and to monitor livestock after detonation.

Testing of Soil, Hay or Feedstuffs

Although the risk is low, and we may not have data on pre-existing soil concentrations to compare to after event testing, there is optional testing available to measure lead in soil, hay and feedstuffs. Mental Health

The Missouri Department of Mental Health's Office of Disaster Services is working with partner agencies to support Missourians as the situation develops. Feeling anxious or afraid when facing the unknown is common. Help is available any time:

  • Disaster Distress Helpline: National hotline available 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Call or text 800-985-5990
  • Missouri 988: Missouri hotline available 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Call, text or chat at 988.

SEMA

The Missouri State Emergency Management Agency, a division of the Missouri Department of Public Safety, supports coordination across state agencies. The governor announced activation of the State Emergency Operations Center to support a quick response. The agency's regional coordinators work with local emergency management agencies to address any resource needs that may be identified. SEMA will share additional information about potentially affected areas in Missouri as it becomes available. This information will be provided below.

Claims

May I file a claim if my property is damaged? 

Answer: Yes, you can file a claim against the U.S. Army for any alleged damage done because of the detonation. Claims would likely be considered under both the Federal Tort Claims Act, 28, U.S.C. 1346(b); 2671-2680 and the Military Claims Act (MCA), 10 U.S.C. § 2733. You have two (2) years from the date of incident to file a claim. 

How do I file a claim? 

Answer: Claimants are responsible for correctly completing a Standard Form 95 (SF95) and for furnishing the evidence necessary to substantiate a claim. Fill out each line completely, (NA where it is not applicable), sign, date and list a sum certain.

Where do I file a claim? 

Answer: To file a claim against the U.S. Army, contact the Ft. Leavenworth, Kansas Claims Office: Office of the Staff Judge Advocate 415 Custer Avenue Fort Leavenworth, KS 66027-2313 or by telephone at 913-684-3564.

What is the extent that I can file a claim for? 

Answer: You may file a claim for any damage you believe was caused by the detonation. Make sure you list a sum certain on the SF 95 and provide all required information See SF 95 Instructions.

For more information call 520-693-1317, email usarmy.ria.asc.list.pa@army.mil, or visit the US Army's Community Notifications webpage. For additional information, please review the U.S. Army's Questions and Answers factsheet.
