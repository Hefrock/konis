EPA’s highest priority is the health and safety of all Americans. EPA Region 7 Administrator Jim Macy sent a letter to Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds regarding the U.S. Army’s planned detonation of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant scheduled for Monday, November 23, 2026.

 

EPA, HHS, and DOW are working together to ensure that the controlled detonation is conducted in the safest manner possible, and we are prepared to take the most extreme mitigation measures available to ensure there is no risk to the public and any disruptions are minimized.

November 18:

U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has issued an Emergency Permit under Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) to the U.S. Army for its planned detonation of 68 drums of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant. EPA issued the Emergency Permit based upon the determination that the explosion risk from the degraded condition of the lead azide presented an imminent and substantial threat to human health and the environment. 

 

EPA’s Emergency Permit requires the Army to take concrete actions before, during, and after the detonation for protection of human health and the environment. Specifically, the Army must provide prior public notification of potentially affected areas; conduct pre- and post-detonation sampling and monitoring activities; implement cleanup actions for contaminated areas; and report on each of these activities to the EPA.

 

The Army has provided EPA information documenting that recrystallization of the lead azide in long-term storage has occurred. The recrystallization of lead azide has created a risk of spontaneous explosion due to the material’s sensitivity to friction and shock, creating a highly unstable and dangerous situation. The Army, which has unparalleled expertise in matters concerning explosives safety, concluded that this material cannot safely be handled or moved and therefore, cannot be treated by any method other than in-place detonation.

 

In-place detonation allows for minimal human interaction with the containers of waste lead azide, enables the Army to conduct the treatment activity during favorable weather conditions, and allows the Army the opportunity to provide prior notifications to the potentially affected surrounding communities. Treatment of the lead azide under the Emergency Permit helps ensure maximal control of the conditions leading up to the detonation, including prior public notification of exposure risks that may result from this treatment activity.

 

For more information, including the U.S. Army’s public advisory, question and answers on this planned operation, and their environmental measures to meet EPA’s permit requirements, please visit the Army's website.
