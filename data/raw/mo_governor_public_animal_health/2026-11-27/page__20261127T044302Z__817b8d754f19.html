State of Missouri Acting to Ensure Public and Animal Health in Preparation for U.S. Army's Planned Ammunition Detonation in Iowa

November 20, 2026
Jefferson City

Earlier today, Governor Mike Kehoe activated a multi-state agency team and implemented a comprehensive plan for monitoring air, water, agriculture, and public health in response to the U.S. Army’s planned detonation of materials at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP) on Monday, which could potentially affect northeast Missouri.

“In preparation for Monday’s planned detonation in our neighboring state, my office and our state agency leaders are in communication with environmental and health officials in Iowa, along with federal officials, to ensure we have a response plan, if necessary,” said Governor Kehoe. “While we understand the Army’s need to address these volatile materials, we want to ensure that Missourians have the facts on potential impacts and the work underway by state agencies to prepare.”

The U.S. Army is currently scheduled to perform a controlled detonation of degraded lead azide at IAAAP in Middletown, Iowa, on Monday, November 23. Middletown is 57 miles north of the Missouri state line and 105 miles from Hannibal.

“We are consulting with the National Weather Service, whose forecast calls for light winds out of the north for Monday, which should dramatically minimize risks through the air in northeast Missouri. To ensure a quick response, I am activating the State Emergency Operations Center on Monday," continued Governor Kehoe. 

The Missouri Department of Natural Resources (DNR) is working closely with state and federal partners to assess any post-detonation environmental impacts and to be prepared to respond accordingly. The department is prepared to sample the Mississippi River and any other surface water drinking water sources and provide assistance if necessary.

The Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services (DHSS) Lead Program is monitoring the potential health risk to Missouri residents and is in consultation with federal health partners. While there is minimal risk to people outside of the detonation area, DHSS advises those concerned about exposure to stay indoors as much as possible following detonation.

 The Missouri Department of Agriculture (MDA) stands ready to partner with industry stakeholders to assist producers that may be impacted and ensure Missouri’s food supply remains safe for consumers. MDA advises that livestock producers work with their veterinarians to monitor livestock that may be impacted.

The Missouri Department of Mental Health – Office of Disaster Services is working with partner agencies to support Missourians as the situation develops. When facing the unknown, it’s common to feel anxious or afraid. The Disaster Distress Helpline is available 24/7 at 1-800-985-5990. You can also call, text or chat Missouri 988.

The Missouri State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) will continue to support coordination across state agencies and SEMA’s regional coordinators will continue to work with local emergency management agencies to address any resource needs that may be identified. SEMA will share additional information on potentially affected areas as it becomes available.

Continue to monitor the U.S. Army's updates, including up-to-date maps as they become available at: www.army.mil/asc#org-iowa-army-ammunition-plant.
